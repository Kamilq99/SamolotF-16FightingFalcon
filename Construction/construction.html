The aircraft’s design was the first to intentionally incorporate aerodynamic instability, which is controlled via a computer-based fly-by-wire system. This allows the jet to perform extreme 9g maneuvers with great ease. The demand for instability was introduced through the Energy Maneuverability Theory, developed by former fighter pilots including Colonel John Boyd, who gained experience during the Korean War. This theory revolutionized how maneuverability was achieved during continuous aerial movements.

The practical implementation of this theory in a new aircraft led to what became the F-16 — a design that offered groundbreaking agility at the time. The first flight of the prototype, labeled YF-16, took place on February 2, 1974. After several design and equipment updates, it entered mass production in 1976 in both single- and two-seat variants.

The first units were delivered to the U.S. Air Force in January 1979. Since then, the aircraft has continued evolving. The C and D variants were introduced in the 1980s and eventually replaced all earlier models by the early 1990s. In 1993, General Dynamics sold its aircraft division to Lockheed Corporation. By 1995, Lockheed had merged with Martin Marietta to form Lockheed Martin — the manufacturer of all F-16s delivered to Poland.
